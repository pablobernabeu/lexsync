## Suggested analysis

- **Model:** `response ~ condition + (1 + condition | subject) + (1 | item)` -- where the response is reaction time from the onset of `{target}`; responses collected for 2800 ms; a response ends the trial.
- Crossed random effects for subjects and items guard against the language-as-fixed-effect fallacy (Clark, 1973; Baayen et al., 2008). The condition column codes lexicality, word or pseudoword. Begin with this maximal structure (Barr et al., 2013). If the model is singular or fails to converge, simplify it as Matuschek et al. (2017) describe. The formula is lme4 syntax, for lme4 in R or pymer4 in Python; statsmodels MixedLM cannot take it directly and needs the random effects restated in its own arguments. The equivalence tests in the realised control are post-selection diagnostics on deterministically selected items, not inferential tests over a sample.

## Methods paragraph

60 real English words and 60 length-matched pseudowords (generated by subsyllabic constituent swap (Wuggy-style, deterministic pseudowords)) were assembled for a lexical-decision contrast. Words and pseudowords were matched on length only, and the words differed from the pseudowords in bigram frequency (d = -1.60, 90% CI [-1.94, -1.25]), N (d = 0.32, 90% CI [0.01, 0.62]) and OLD20 (d = -0.34, 90% CI [-0.65, -0.04]). Two one-sided tests at a bound of 0.5 SD (alpha 0.05) supported equivalence on 1 of 1 matched comparisons. The smallest condition was selected from 8177 eligible candidates, and the selection was deterministic and blind to any outcome measure. Materials were counterbalanced into 1 list(s) (a factorial split) and generated for PsychoPy, OpenSesame and jsPsych. Responses were collected from target onset until 2800 ms after it, and a response ended the trial. The selection is deterministic and reproducible (lexsync 0.1.1), and the seed (2026) fixes the trial order.
